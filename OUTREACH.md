# Outreach and participation policy

Updated 2026-09-05. This replaces the earlier generic invitation templates and automatic follow-up sequence. The [30-day plan](OUTREACH-ATTRACTION-PLAN.md) and [named organization review](docs/participation-partners-2026-09-05.md) provide the current offers and draft messages.

## Before contact

1. Read prior contact history in [outreach-log.md](outreach-log.md) and any available working log. Distinguish simulation from confirmed delivery. A closed pitch or silence is not an invitation to try a new channel.
2. Finish a useful, source-linked artifact: a correction packet, locally reviewed role brief, or genuine contribution through the organization's own process. Use verified, dated claims and show uncertainty.
3. Check the organization's current public contribution route and data terms. Do not guess personal contacts, scrape addresses, or use a bug tracker for a partnership pitch.
4. Name the human sender accurately. Ask one small, optional question. Never require endorsement, reciprocal publicity, account creation, or ideological agreement to use the work.
5. Have a human review the actual recipient, channel, text, and artifact. Agents prepare local drafts. Sending or posting requires explicit authorization; a planning request is not permission to contact organizations.

## Message and follow-up rules

- Keep a first message short, usually under 120 words. Lead with the completed artifact and why it may help the recipient's existing work. Do not promise integration, funding, free staff time, or reach the project has not established.
- Use a prior relationship or a community's explicitly permitted route when available. Avoid bulk campaigns and duplicate channels.
- No scheduled or automatic follow-up. A later message requires a material new contribution or a recipient invitation plus human review and authorization. Honor decline, opt-out, and silence.
- Introductions require both parties' agreement. Ask separately before sharing nonpublic correspondence or publishing a collaboration story. A citation, reply, correction, or permission to reuse data is not partnership approval.
- Treat source terms and contributor privacy as part of the artifact. Never call all ingested data CC0 merely because it is publicly readable.

## Record the outcome accurately

Keep the historic log intact; append dated corrections rather than silently rewriting events. The working May log includes both dry-run and live entries and needs reconciliation before calculating response rates.

For each attempt record: organization, human owner, prior-contact review, artifact/version, intended channel, authorization reference, and status. Use `draft`, `ready_for_review`, `authorized`, `delivered`, `delivery_unknown`, `replied`, `declined`, or `closed`; keep simulations separate. Record delivery evidence before setting `delivered`, and a concrete accepted correction/use/introduction before counting traction. Store only a minimal public summary; keep private contact details and messages out of public issues.

## Participation is broader than outreach

People can [correct a listing or contribute evidence](participate.html), use a brief independently, improve a source directory, translate terms, test discovery, or challenge a framework claim. Organization representation and factual review are separate checks; neither grants endorsement or governance rights. Formal affiliations and governance changes follow [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md).
