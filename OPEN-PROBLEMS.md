# Open problems: working positions and tests

Written 2026-11-26. This page takes each open question in the [README](README.md#open-questions), the three [tensions](README.md#tensions-and-tradeoffs), and the structural problems filed as issues, and gives each one the same treatment:

- **Working position.** What the project should assume for now. It is a default to argue with, not a settled answer.
- **Evidence.** Sources that were opened and read on 2026-11-26 by an automated research pass. No person has re-checked them yet.
- **Against.** The strongest counter-evidence found.
- **Test.** Something a third party could observe that would change the position.
- **Organizations.** Which [solution circuit](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md) puts the position into practice, if any.
- **Still needed.** What writing cannot supply.

None of these problems is solved. Several positions below are weaker than the claims they replace, and that is the main result. Where an older document in this repository states a figure that the sources do not support, the [corrections](#corrections-to-earlier-documents) section says so.

## Developing nations

*How do you transition a globalized economy without leaving developing nations worse off?*

**Working position.** Do not assume poorer countries can follow the export-manufacturing route that earlier industrializers used. Plan around three facts: that route is closing, money sent home by migrants is now the largest external income source, and direct cash has measurable local benefits. The parts of the framework most likely to help are worker and producer ownership, which keep more income local, and member finance.

**Evidence.**
- Countries are running out of industrialization opportunities sooner and at much lower income levels than early industrializers did. Rodrik, [Premature Deindustrialization](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20935).
- About 40% of global employment is exposed to AI: roughly 60% in advanced economies, 40% in emerging markets and 26% in low-income countries. [IMF, January 2024](https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2024/01/14/ai-will-transform-the-global-economy-lets-make-sure-it-benefits-humanity).
- Remittances to low- and middle-income countries reached an estimated $656 billion in 2023, more than foreign direct investment and official aid. [World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2024/06/26/remittances-slowed-in-2023-expected-to-grow-faster-in-2024).
- One-off transfers of about $1,000 to over 10,500 Kenyan households produced a local multiplier of 2.5 with minimal price inflation. [Egger et al., Econometrica 2022](https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/2022/11/01/General-Equilibrium-Effects-of-Cash-Transfers-Experimental-Evidence-From-Kenya).
- India's Self Employed Women's Association is a union that has promoted more than 100 member-owned cooperatives. [ILO](https://www.ilo.org/resource/article/sewa-cooperative-federation-india-towards-self-reliance).

**Against.** Rodrik also finds that Asian manufacturing exporters were largely insulated, so the pattern is not universal. Lower AI exposure means less disruption but also less ability to capture gains. The Kenyan transfers were paid for from outside the local economy, so they say nothing about a tax-financed scheme. Fair trade evidence is "mixed and incomplete" and causality is not established ([Dragusanu, Giovannucci and Nunn](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20357/w20357.pdf)). Kerala's Kudumbashree network has 4.3 million members, yet a state audit found at least 35% of its micro-enterprises inactive ([Comptroller and Auditor General, 2018](https://www.cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2017/Chapter_3_Local_Self_Government_Department_of_Report_No_4_of_2018_-_General_and_Social_Sector_Government_of_Kerala.pdf)).

**Test.** If remittance flows to low-income countries fall for three consecutive years while automation-exposed sectors in host countries shed migrant jobs, this position underestimates the harm and the question needs a transfer mechanism between countries, not only local ownership.

**Organizations.** [Worker ownership transition](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#worker-ownership-transition), [Local food security loop](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#local-food-security-loop).

**Still needed.** A co-author working in a lower-income country. The directory itself is 81.6% Brazil and Australia by row count, and the map is 91.3% United States and United Kingdom ([data audit](docs/DATA-AUDIT-2026-09-05.md)), so it cannot yet test anything here.

## Nation states

*What role do existing nation-states play? Do they dissolve, federate, or transform?*

**Working position.** They stay. Every circuit in the catalogue lists something only a public body can provide: a purchase right, a reimbursement rate, a dispatch system, a feed-in price. The practical question is which level of government to work with. National law sets what is possible; municipal and regional practice is where most documented change happened.

**Evidence.**
- Kerala devolved 35 to 40 percent of plan expenditure to local governments in a single legislative act and convened popular assemblies. Heller, [Moving the State](https://courses.washington.edu/pbaf531/Heller_MovingtheState.pdf), 2001.
- Brazil passed a national solidarity economy law in December 2024, [Lei 15.068](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2023-2026/2024/lei/l15068.htm).
- Italian law requires affiliated cooperatives to put 3% of profits into funds that finance new cooperatives. [Law 59/1992](https://www.diritto.it/i-fondi-mutualistici-per-la-promozione-e-lo-sviluppo-della-cooperazione/).
- In Scotland 554 community groups own 214,883 hectares, 2.8% of the land. [Scottish Government, 2026](https://www.gov.scot/publications/community-ownership-in-scotland-2025/pages/key-points/).

**Against.** Enabling law does not guarantee use. The US Main Street Employee Ownership Act of 2018 left the main financing barrier in place ([NCBA CLUSA](https://us.coop/blog/new-sba-report-on-financing-alternatives-does-not-fulfill-congresss-directive-in-main-street-employee-ownership-act/)). Scottish community ownership is concentrated: 72% of the area is in the Western Isles. Wales's future-generations law "is not driving the system-wide change that was intended" after ten years ([Audit Wales, 2025](https://audit.wales/news/ten-years-well-being-future-generations-act-has-increased-prominence-not-driving-system-wide)).

**Test.** If, across five countries with enabling cooperative or commons law, uptake is no higher than in comparable countries without it, the law-first position is wrong and effort belongs elsewhere.

**Organizations.** [Participatory budgeting](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#participatory-budgeting), [Anchor procurement and community wealth](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#anchor-procurement-and-community-wealth).

**Still needed.** A comparative study of what happens to local commons institutions when national government turns hostile. Nothing checked here covers that.

## Power vacuum

*How do you prevent a post-transfer power vacuum from being filled by authoritarians?*

**Working position.** The evidence points to two conditions after a political transition: people stay mobilized, and they direct that energy into building and using new institutions instead of continued all-or-nothing confrontation. This is the opposite of both demobilizing and escalating.

**Evidence.**
- Democratic regimes that came out of nonviolent resistance survive substantially longer than those that did not. Bayer, Bethke and Lambach, [Journal of Peace Research 2016](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:joupea:v:53:y:2016:i:6:p:758-771).
- Across all political transitions from 1945 to 2011, democratic outcomes depended on sustaining high mobilization and steering it away from maximalist goals. Pinckney, [From Dissent to Democracy](https://www.prio.org/publications/12447), 2020.
- Communities with more organizations focused on local life had larger falls in violence. [Sharkey et al., 2017](https://inequality.stanford.edu/node/9575).

**Against.** Both transition studies are observational. External intervention can override domestic conditions: a US Senate committee found no evidence of direct US involvement in the 1973 Chilean coup, but documented a 1970 attempt to foment one and $8 million of covert spending between 1970 and 1973 ([Church Committee staff report](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/sites/default/files/94chile.pdf)). Since 2010 fewer than 34% of nonviolent campaigns have succeeded, down from as high as 65% in the 1990s ([Chenoweth, 2020](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/the-future-of-nonviolent-resistance-2/)).

**Test.** If a new dataset of post-2011 transitions shows no relationship between institution-building mobilization and democratic survival, drop this position.

**Organizations.** [Disaster resilience grid](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#disaster-resilience-grid) is the nearest practical rehearsal: organizations that already know each other before a shock.

**Still needed.** THEORY-OF-CHANGE.md still has no answer to sustained armed repression or foreign intervention. This page does not supply one.

## Historical wrongs

*What does justice look like for historical wrongs without creating new cycles of resentment?*

**Working position.** Truth-telling and material repair do different jobs, and programs that delivered only one are the ones recorded as falling short. Repair has precedents with defined eligibility, a fixed sum and an end date.

**Evidence.**
- The United States paid $20,000 each to more than 82,219 Japanese American claimants, over $1.6 billion in total, closing the program in 1999. [US National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/research/japanese-americans/redress).
- West Germany agreed in 1952 to pay 3 billion Deutschmarks to Israel and 450 million to the Claims Conference. [German History in Documents and Images](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/occupation-and-the-emergence-of-two-states-1945-1961/communique-regarding-restitution-for-israel-and-the-jews-september-10-1952).
- In South Africa, people who accepted the commission's account of the past were more likely to hold reconciled attitudes. [Gibson, 2004](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:amposc:v:48:y:2004:i:2:p:201-217).

**Against.** South Africa paid a once-off R30,000 to 16,837 approved applicants ([Department of Justice, 2014](https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/justice-truth-and-reconciliation-commission-recommendations-identified)) while close to R1.9 billion sat unspent in the reparations fund in 2022 ([GroundUp](https://groundup.org.za/article/unspent-money-the-r19-billion-fund-meant-for-apartheid-reparations)). Rwanda's gacaca courts heard about 1.96 million cases ([JusticeInfo](https://www.justiceinfo.net/en/40846-rwanda-the-most-judged-genocide-in-history.html)); Human Rights Watch called the result "a mixed success" with fair-trial violations and cases used to settle scores ([2011](https://www.hrw.org/report/2011/05/31/justice-compromised/legacy-rwandas-community-based-gacaca-courts)). Evanston's local reparations program is being challenged in federal court, with the Justice Department allowed to join the case in October 2026 ([WBEZ](https://www.wbez.org/race-class-communities/2026/10/23/doj-allowed-to-join-lawsuit-against-evanston-reparations-program-for-black-residents)). The Caribbean Community's [ten-point plan](https://caricomreparations.org/caricom/caricoms-10-point-reparation-plan/) has produced no payments.

**Test.** If surveys after a completed repair program show resentment rising among non-recipients more than trust rises among recipients, the "new cycles of resentment" worry is real for that design.

**Organizations.** [Land stewardship alliance](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#land-stewardship-alliance), with the care note there: Indigenous nations are rights-holders, not a role in someone else's plan.

**Still needed.** A practitioner co-author. This entry is a reading of published sources by an outsider.

## Principled refusers

*How do you handle people who genuinely don't want to participate in collective governance?*

**Working position.** Ostrom-style sanctions address people who take without contributing. A person who refuses on principle is different, and existing law has handled that by pairing the exemption with a waiver: you may stop paying if you also stop receiving, and if your community demonstrably looks after its own.

**Evidence.**
- US law lets members of a recognized religious sect opposed to insurance apply for exemption from the self-employment tax, provided they waive all benefits and the sect makes provision for its dependent members. [26 U.S.C. § 1402(g)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1402).
- The Supreme Court allowed Amish families to end formal schooling after the eighth grade, citing their sincerity, self-sufficiency and alternative vocational education. [Wisconsin v. Yoder, 1972](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/406/205).

**Against.** Both exemptions are narrow and religious. The Yoder opinion says a philosophical and personal choice, like Thoreau's, would not qualify, and one justice dissented in part over whether the children's own wishes were heard. An exit that parents choose for children is not consent. Universal entitlements cannot be waived on a child's behalf under the framework's own first principle.

**Test.** If a community that has opted out of a shared system shows worse outcomes for its children or dependent adults than the surrounding population, the exemption is harming people who did not choose it and should be narrowed.

**Organizations.** None. This is a rule question.

**Still needed.** Any evidence from non-religious cases, and a position on refusal that harms others, such as refusing vaccination or ecological limits.

## Prisons

*What replaces prisons? What does accountability look like without punishment?*

**Working position.** Nothing checked here supports "replace". The evidence supports using prison less in specific situations: restorative conferencing where both sides agree, paid work straight after release, and health-led response to crisis calls. For serious violence, the largest trial found no significant effect on its main measure.

**Evidence.**
- Restorative conferencing: a modest but cost-effective reduction in repeat offending and higher victim satisfaction across ten randomized trials. [Strang et al., 2013](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:camsys:v:9:y:2013:i:1:p:1-59).
- Paid transitional jobs: 16-22% lower recidivism for people enrolling within three months of release. [MDRC, 2012](https://www.mdrc.org/work/publications/more-job).
- Health responders: a 34% fall in reports of targeted minor crimes in Denver's pilot. [Dee and Pyne, 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9176742).
- A youth restorative program in San Francisco: rearrest about 20 percentage points lower at six months in a randomized trial of 143 cases. [California Policy Lab, 2022](https://capolicylab.org/news/report-san-francisco-juvenile-restorative-justice-program-reduces-probability-of-rearrest/).

**Against.** The READI Chicago trial of jobs plus therapy for men at very high risk found no statistically significant change in serious violence after 20 months ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30852)). Violence-interruption evidence is "mixed at best" ([Butts et al., 2015](https://jeffreybutts.net/2014/12/30/arph2015/)). The conferencing trials only included willing parties. CAHOOTS, the longest-running crisis service, stopped serving Eugene in April 2025 for lack of funds ([OPB](https://opb.org/article/2025/04/08/cahoots-service-ending-in-eugene-effective-immediately)). Cross-country recidivism comparisons "are currently not valid" ([Fazel and Wolf, 2015](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0130390)).

**Test.** If a jurisdiction that expands diversion for a class of offences sees repeat harm rise relative to a matched jurisdiction over three years, narrow the class.

**Organizations.** [Community safety and repair](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#community-safety-and-repair), [Non-police crisis response](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#non-police-crisis-response).

**Still needed.** An answer for people who are dangerous and unwilling. And a practitioner co-author, as the README already says.

## AI governance

*How do you govern AI development during and after the transition?*

**Working position.** Separate consultation from constraint. Companies and governments have run public-input exercises, and no source checked shows one changing a specific decision. The limits that bind so far came from statute and from collective bargaining. Community organizations can add local oversight law. So the route for this framework is unions, legislatures and local ordinances, with deliberative input feeding them.

**Evidence.**
- The EU AI Act, [Regulation 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202401689), entered into force in August 2024 with staged application.
- The Writers Guild's 2023 agreement bars companies from requiring writers to use AI and from treating AI output as source material. [WGA summary](https://www.wgacontract2023.org/the-campaign/summary-of-the-2023-wga-mba).
- Local surveillance-oversight laws are reported in 26 US jurisdictions. [ACLU](https://www.aclu.org/community-control-over-police-surveillance), self-reported.
- Public-input exercises exist at some scale: about 1,000 Americans in [Anthropic's 2023 experiment](https://www.anthropic.com/research/collective-constitutional-ai-aligning-a-language-model-with-public-input), 1,545 people across four countries in [Meta's forum with Stanford](https://deliberation.stanford.edu/meta-community-forum-generative-ai-results), and Taiwan's [Alignment Assemblies](https://moda.gov.tw/en/major-policies/alignment-assemblies/1453).

**Against.** The dates for the AI Act's high-risk obligations were pushed back in 2026, to December 2027 for the main category ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/07/EU-AI-Act-Update-Digital-Omnibus-Finalizes-8-Compliance-Changes), a law-firm note; the official text was not located). A study of one public-input program finds unspoken assumptions that limit what participants can decide ([Moats and Ganguly](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11613)). A data cooperative for gig workers has been wound down ([Princeton](https://wao.cs.princeton.edu/)). Citizens' recommendations can be ignored: the Wikimedia community approved a movement charter by 73% and the Foundation's board declined to ratify it ([Wikimedia, 2024](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Movement_Charter/Drafting_Committee/Announcement_-_Results_of_the_ratification_vote)).

**Test.** If, by the end of 2028, no documented case exists of a deliberative public input changing a deployed AI system or a binding rule, stop listing deliberation as a governance mechanism and list it as research.

**Organizations.** [Community oversight of technology](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#community-oversight-of-technology).

**Still needed.** A maintainer who follows this field. This entry will date quickly. Disclosure: it was drafted by an AI system made by one of the companies named above.

## Cultural diversity

*How do you maintain cultural and individual diversity in a system designed for collective wellbeing?*

**Working position.** Guarantee two things everywhere, the right to leave and a floor of entitlements, and leave the rest to local rule-making. This is the same position as [local exclusion](#local-exclusion) seen from the other side.

**Evidence.** Reviewing 91 studies, researchers found Ostrom's design principles well supported, including the principle that rules should fit local conditions. [Cox, Arnold and Villamayor-Tomás, 2010](https://www.ecologyandsociety.org/vol15/iss4/art38/).

**Against.** The same review records the criticism that the principles treat communities as "coherent wholes without internal conflict or heterogeneity". Union-built housing has produced homogeneous enclaves ([Urban Omnibus on Electchester](https://urbanomnibus.net/2013/12/electchester-a-city-made-for-workers/)), and strong local networks can exclude outsiders from disaster help ([review of Aldrich](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/lsereviewofbooks/2013/02/14/book-review-building-resilience-social-capital-in-post-disaster-recovery)).

**Test.** In any pilot, compare who uses a commons with who lives in the area. A persistent gap for a minority group means local adaptation is working as exclusion.

**Organizations.** [Community school hub](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#community-school-hub).

**Still needed.** Anthropological evidence. Thin here.

## Spirituality

*What is the role of spirituality, religion, and personal belief systems?*

**Working position.** No special role and no exclusion. Faith communities are participants on the same terms as others, and they are already a large share of the directory's food, care and housing records. The accommodation in [principled refusers](#principled-refusers) applies.

**Evidence.** Only the legal accommodations cited above were checked. The directory shows the practical point: for example, the eldercare and food-access role rules match many church-run pantries and homes.

**Against.** No source was checked on whether faith-based provision excludes people or attaches conditions. That is a known concern and it is untested here.

**Test.** Sample 50 faith-run service records and check published eligibility rules. If more than a fifth restrict access by belief or conduct, the directory should label that.

**Organizations.** None specific.

**Still needed.** Someone from sociology of religion. This is the least developed entry.

## Ideology drift

*How do you keep this framework from becoming the very kind of rigid ideology it seeks to replace?*

**Working position.** The safeguard is procedural and already exists in part: claims carry falsifiers, and a failed test changes the text. What was missing was anyone running the tests. This page and the circuit catalogue ran some, and several claims lost.

**Evidence.** See [corrections](#corrections-to-earlier-documents): six figures in existing documents did not survive a source check. Kibbutzim are the long-run case of an ideal revised under pressure. The author's own summary shows roughly 85% had moved away from full equal sharing, mostly from the late 1990s ([Abramitzky slides](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~saez/course/Abramitzky_book_presentation.pdf); the book chapter was not opened).

**Against.** Self-correction by the same people who wrote the claims is weak. The sample check of role rules in the circuit catalogue was done by one AI reviewer, not blind.

**Test.** FALSIFIERS.md F-PROJ-5 already applies: fewer than 10 external contributors with merged changes after 12 months means the correction loop is one person and their tools.

**Organizations.** [Civic information commons](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#civic-information-commons), which is itself an untested idea.

**Still needed.** Outside reviewers.

## Leaderless coordination

*How does a fully open, leaderless network coordinate action at global scale without becoming either chaotic or quietly hierarchical?*

**Working position.** It does not. Stop using "leaderless". Peer-production communities become more oligarchic as they grow, so the realistic aim is named, elected, replaceable roles and a published way to overrule them.

**Evidence.**
- Across 683 wikis, growth was associated with increasing oligarchy, supporting Michels' iron law. [Shaw and Hill, 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.0323).
- Freeman's seven principles of democratic structuring start with delegating specific authority to specific people by democratic procedure and making them answerable. [The Tyranny of Structurelessness](https://www.jofreeman.com/joreen/tyranny.htm).
- Debian elects its project leader and lets developers override decisions by general resolution. [Debian Constitution](https://www.debian.org/devel/constitution).

**Against.** Formal structure does not settle who decides: the Wikimedia community voted for a charter and the board declined it. Patient capture of an open project is real: an attacker spent over two years becoming a trusted maintainer of the xz compression library before inserting a backdoor ([NVD](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2024-3094), [timeline](https://research.swtch.com/xz-timeline)). Evidence that rotation or sortition reduces oligarchy is thin; a 2026 review says "there is very little empirical evidence" ([Cirone and Van Coppenolle](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/9130715E636DB75989350B02835CE018/core-reader)), and term limits in US state legislatures shifted power to governors and lobbyists ([Carey et al., 2006](https://experts.boisestate.edu/en/publications/the-effects-of-term-limits-on-state-legislatures-a-new-survey-of-/)).

**Test.** Measure the share of merged changes approved by the single most active maintainer each quarter. If it stays above 80% once there are five or more regular contributors, the governance document describes a structure that does not exist.

**Organizations.** [Participatory budgeting](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#participatory-budgeting).

**Still needed.** GOVERNANCE.md should drop rotation as an assumed remedy until someone finds evidence for it in organizations like this one.

## Transparency limits

*If transparency is the security model, what are its failure modes? When is openness genuinely dangerous?*

**Working position.** Already largely adopted in the README: be open about power, money and decisions, and protective of people. This page adds one operational rule, applied in the circuit catalogue: where work involves people at risk, publish country-level counts only and never city-level lists.

**Evidence.** The September audit found 214 release rows matching invitation-link patterns and 4,145 mutual aid rows copied from a 2020 wiki ([data audit](docs/DATA-AUDIT-2026-09-05.md)). Openness also failed as a defence in the xz case above, where everything was public and the attack still nearly worked.

**Against.** Withholding city-level data makes the tool less useful for exactly the organizers who need it. There is no evidence here on where the right line is.

**Test.** Ask ten organizations in sensitive categories whether they want to be findable at city level. If most say yes, the default is too cautious.

**Organizations.** Applies to Housing First, crisis response, safety and repair, community schools and land stewardship in the catalogue.

**Still needed.** Input from people organizing under repression.

## Tipping point

*What happens if the technological tipping point arrives before the network is ready?*

**Working position.** Past shocks say the damage is local and lasting, and that retraining helps for a while. So the useful preparation is the ability to act within weeks when a specific employer closes: an organized workforce, conversion advice and money that moves fast. Whether AI produces such a shock is uncertain, and this position does not depend on it.

**Evidence.**
- Job and income losses in US manufacturing communities hit by import competition persisted for years. [Autor, Dorn and Hanson, 2021](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/on-the-persistence-of-the-china-shock/).
- Displaced workers approved for trade adjustment help earned $50,000 more over ten years, yet annual returns had fully faded by the end. [Hyman, 2022](https://www.census.gov/library/working-papers/2022/adrm/CES-WP-22-05.html).
- Spain's 2018 coal agreement between government and unions provided €250 million and early retirement for about 60% of miners. [IndustriALL](https://www.industriall-union.org/spanish-coal-unions-win-landmark-just-transition-deal).
- A model of robot taxation finds a tax is optimal only while current routine workers are still in the labor force. [Guerreiro, Rebelo and Teles](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23806).

**Against.** Italian worker buyouts, the best rapid-response precedent, total a few hundred firms in thirty years. Germany's managed end to hard coal still saw dismissals in 2019 that workers challenged in court ([LTO](https://www.lto.de/recht/nachrichten/n/arbg-gelsenkirchen-kuendigungen-kuendigungsschutzklage-zeche-prosper-haniel-bergleute-demonstration-ruhrgebiet-kohle)). Denmark's flexible labor market "places a heavy burden on active labor market policies" and risks marginalizing low-educated workers ([Andersen, 2015](https://wol.iza.org/articles/flexicurity-labor-market-during-recession/long)).

**Test.** Count worker buyouts completed within twelve months of a closure announcement in one region. If there are none over three years despite closures, the rapid-response capacity does not exist there.

**Organizations.** [Worker ownership transition](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#worker-ownership-transition). The catalogue shows no city in the release with a match for all four of its roles, and cooperative lenders are the role most often missing.

**Still needed.** The framework still has no answer if displacement is fast and general. That contingency is unaddressed.

## Ecological limits

*Democratic Sovereignty vs. Ecological Equilibrium: who defines planetary boundaries, and who enforces them when democratic institutions disagree?*

**Working position.** No single body does this, and the README's claim that limits are "pre-political" overstates it. Three imperfect mechanisms exist and work better together: constitutional courts that protect future people's freedoms, statutory guardians who speak for a place or for future generations, and secure community tenure. Each has failed alone.

**Evidence.**
- Scientists assess six of nine planetary boundaries as transgressed ([Richardson et al., 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10499318/)), and seven by a 2025 update ([Stockholm Resilience Centre](https://www.stockholmresilience.org/news--events/general-news/2025-09-24-seven-of-nine-planetary-boundaries-now-breached)).
- Germany's constitutional court held in 2021 that pushing emission cuts past 2030 offloads burdens onto later freedoms, and parliament tightened targets within weeks. [Court press release](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/EN/2021/bvg21-031.html).
- New Zealand made the Whanganui River a legal person with two appointed guardians. [Te Awa Tupua Act 2017](https://ecojurisprudence.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/New-Zealand_Te-Awa-Tupua-Act-2017_127.pdf) (a mirror of the Act).
- Ecuador's constitutional court barred mining in the Los Cedros forest on rights-of-nature grounds. [Decision, unofficial translation](https://celdf.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/Los-Cedros-Decision-ENGLISH-Final.pdf).
- Titling Indigenous communities in Peru cut forest clearing by more than three-quarters in the short term. [Blackman et al., 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5402456/).
- International agreement has worked once at this scale: ozone is expected to return to 1980 values around 2066 over the Antarctic. [WMO/UNEP, 2022](https://ozone.unep.org/system/files/documents/Scientific-Assessment-of-Ozone-Depletion-2022-Executive-Summary.pdf).

**Against.** The German ruling rested on freedom over time; the court rejected the claim that the state had failed a duty to protect. Wales's commissioner can only recommend, with a duty on bodies to comply or explain, and the 2019 decision against a motorway cited cost and environmental harm without crediting the Act ([decision letter](https://www.gov.wales/sites/default/files/publications/2019-06/m4-corridor-around-newport-decision-letter.pdf)). Citizens' assemblies are advisory; Ireland's 2018 referendum passed with 1,429,981 votes to 723,632 ([official result](https://www.referendum.ie/archive/referendum-on-the-regulation-of-termination-of-pregnancy-thirty-sixth-amendment-of-the-constitution-bill-2018/)) only after parliament and government chose to act. The review of Ostrom's principles is "uncertain as to whether the principles may apply to systems at a variety of scales".

**Test.** Track the three German, Welsh and New Zealand mechanisms for ten years. If none has blocked a major extraction or infrastructure decision that a government wanted, they are symbolic and the tension is unresolved in practice.

**Organizations.** [Land stewardship alliance](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#land-stewardship-alliance), [Community energy transition](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#community-energy-transition).

**Still needed.** The README sentence "Planetary boundaries are pre-political" should be revisited. The measurements are scientific. Choosing which risks to accept is a political act.

## Unglamorous work

*Voluntary Contribution vs. Universal Sufficiency: who does the necessary work nobody wants?*

**Working position.** Paid workers, with a union and where possible an ownership stake. The documented attempts to cover care through volunteering or time credits shrank once paid public provision arrived. This supports claim C3 and narrows "voluntary contribution" to mean nobody works under threat of destitution.

**Evidence.**
- Cooperative Home Care Associates combines worker ownership with a union contract and reports turnover below comparable agencies. [Rutgers case study](https://cleo.rutgers.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/CASE-STUDY-CHCA.pdf).
- Kibbutzim, the largest voluntary-equality experiment, mostly introduced differential wages with a safety net from the late 1990s. [Abramitzky slides](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~saez/course/Abramitzky_book_presentation.pdf).
- Japan's eldercare time-credit schemes declined after public long-term care insurance began. [Hayashi, 2012](https://www.ijccr.net/article/view/9150).

**Against.** Fewer than half of the home-care co-op's workers are members, and its wages depend on public reimbursement rates. Community health worker programs show a positive return in one analysis, but the underlying differences in hospital use were not statistically significant ([Kangovi et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8564553/)). Nothing here covers sanitation or night work outside care.

**Test.** FALSIFIERS.md F-FW-2 stands: one large care network sustained for ten years on unpaid labor without elevated burnout would weaken this.

**Organizations.** [Care work with a union and a stake](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#care-work-with-a-union-and-a-stake), [Eldercare commons](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#eldercare-commons).

**Still needed.** The README's ordering "automation first, then rotation, then compensation premiums" has no evidence behind rotation. Pay should come first until someone finds some.

## Local exclusion

*Common Ownership vs. Cultural Adaptation: who decides when a community has crossed from adaptation into exclusion?*

**Working position.** Someone outside the community, applying a short fixed list that local rules cannot lower: access to food, shelter, care and safety, and the right to leave. Inside that floor, local rules stand. This is federalism, and it needs an external body, which the framework does not have.

**Evidence.** Shared-equity housing shows a floor and local variation can coexist. Across 58 programs and 4,108 homes, 95% were affordable to households at or below 80% of area median income, and the share of minority households rose from 13% to 43% over three decades ([Wang et al., Lincoln Institute, 2019](https://www.lincolninst.edu/publications/working-papers/tracking-growth-evaluating-performance-shared-equity-homeownership)).

**Against.** Local control has excluded people in documented cases (see [cultural diversity](#cultural-diversity)). Members can also vote a commons out of existence, as at Seward Park ([history](https://sewardparkcoop.com/spc_history.html)), and a 68% vote kept Penn South limited-equity only in exchange for tax relief ([history](https://www.pennsouth.coop/penn-south-history.html)).

**Test.** If a commons institution's member demographics diverge from its catchment area by more than a set margin for five years with no corrective action, the floor is not being enforced.

**Organizations.** [Tenant purchase and anti-displacement pipeline](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#tenant-purchase-and-anti-displacement-pipeline).

**Still needed.** A proposal for who the outside body is. None is offered here.

## Power transfer

*Why would those with power give it up? (Issue #37)*

**Working position.** Mostly they do not, and the honest target is partial transfer that lasts: particular sectors and places where ownership or decision rights moved and stayed moved for decades. The draft question "is there a version that works under partial transfer?" should be answered yes, because that is the only version with precedents.

**Evidence.**
- Union-sponsored cooperative housing has lasted close to a century in New York and Sweden ([Amalgamated](https://amalgamated-bronx.coop/?p=7), [Riksbyggen](https://www.riksbyggen.se/om-riksbyggen/about-us/)).
- In Emilia-Romagna, 3,486 cooperatives employ about 230,000 people, 12.8% of the workforce ([Il Sole 24 Ore, 2026](https://en.ilsole24ore.com/art/emilia-romagna-cooperation-boosts-revenue-and-growth-by-44-per-cent-AJgNTfBC)).
- Preston's anchor institutions more than tripled the share of spending kept in the city ([council case study](https://www.councils.coop/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/Community-Wealth-Building-Preston-City-Council.pdf)).
- Nonviolent campaigns were more than twice as effective as violent ones from 1900 to 2006 ([Chenoweth and Stephan, publisher's summary](https://cup.columbia.edu/book/why-civil-resistance-works/9780231156837/)).

**Against.** Success rates for nonviolent campaigns have fallen since 2010, and the 3.5% participation figure is "a tendency, rather than a law" ([Chenoweth, 2020](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/centers/carr/publications/questions-answers-and-some-cautionary-updates-regarding-35-rule)). Incumbents adapt: a majority of new renewable capacity undercut fossil alternatives by 2019 ([IRENA](https://www.irena.org/-/media/Files/IRENA/Agency/Publication/2020/Jun/IRENA_Costs_2019_EN.pdf)), yet explicit fossil fuel subsidies doubled to $1.3 trillion in 2022 ([IMF](https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2023/08/24/fossil-fuel-subsidies-surged-to-record-7-trillion)). Partial transfers reverse: union housing has been sold to the market and Germany's union housing group collapsed.

**Test.** List every sector-level transfer the framework cites and record whether it still exists 30 years on. If fewer than half do, "durable partial transfer" is also too optimistic.

**Organizations.** [Labor-for-housing pipeline](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#labor-for-housing-pipeline), [Worker ownership transition](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#worker-ownership-transition), [Anchor procurement and community wealth](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#anchor-procurement-and-community-wealth).

**Still needed.** The security and external-intervention gaps listed at the end of THEORY-OF-CHANGE.md remain open.

## Partial abundance

*Post-scarcity is a spectrum: redesign for partial abundance. (Issue #36)*

**Working position.** The README has already adopted this. What the circuit evidence adds is a sharper version: most working examples of meeting needs outside the market run on continuing public money, and they stop when it stops. Design every local plan around the funding it depends on.

**Evidence.** Housing First doubled the share of participants stably housed in Canada's trial ([Mental Health Commission of Canada](https://mentalhealthcommission.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/At2520Home2520Final2520Report2520-2520Exec2520Summary2520-2520ENG_0.pdf)). Community land trust owners had a foreclosure rate of 0.46% at the end of 2010 against 4.63% for the wider market ([Thaden, 2011](https://www.lincolninst.edu/publications/working-papers/stable-home-ownership-turbulent-economy/)).

**Against.** Finland's homelessness rose 20% in 2025 after benefit cuts. Eugene's crisis service closed. UK community energy growth collapsed when the feed-in tariff ended. More than half of US food hubs are highly grant-dependent. Each source is in the [catalogue](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md). The land trust comparison is not matched for borrower characteristics.

**Test.** For each circuit tried locally, record the public funding line it depends on. If a circuit keeps operating two years after losing that line, this position is too pessimistic for it.

**Organizations.** [Housing First partnership](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#housing-first-partnership) and most others.

**Still needed.** Claim C2 in CLAIMS.md should be read against this.

## Land transition

*Land transition requires a values transformation that the framework places later. (Issue #39)*

**Working position.** The circular dependency goes away if the goal is dropped from "all land is commons" to "a growing stock of land held outside the speculative market, acquired parcel by parcel". That is happening without any prior change in values, through purchase rights, public land transfers and trusts.

**Evidence.**
- The United States has 314 community land trust and shared-equity entities with a projected 43,931 homes ([2022 census](https://www.lincolninst.edu/publications/working-papers/2022-census-community-land-trusts-shared-equity-entities-in-united)); England and Wales have 350 trusts ([CLT Network](https://www.communitylandtrusts.org.uk/news-and-events/new-research-shows-potential-for-278000-more-community-led-homes/)).
- Champlain Housing Trust homes sold at a price affordable to households on 56.6% of area median income and resold at 53.4%, so affordability held across resales ([Davis, Boston Fed, 2009](https://www.bostonfed.org/-/media/Documents/cb/PDF/J_Emmeus_Davis_shared_equity.pdf)).
- Washington DC's purchase law is credited with 16,224 affordable units.

**Against.** The scale is small: about 44,000 homes nationally. Only 47 of the surveyed US entities met the classic trust definition. The Caño Martín Peña trust in San Juan had executed 110 surface-rights deeds for about 1,500 households by 2020 ([Algoed et al.](https://www.cltweb.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/CH11.pdf)). Sweden's cooperative housing, 24% of the stock, has traded at market prices since the 1970s ([Cooperative Housing International](https://www.housinginternational.coop/co-ops/sweden/)), so collective form does not mean decommodified land.

**Test.** If the count of price-restricted homes in trusts and limited-equity co-ops is flat or falling over the next two census rounds, parcel-by-parcel growth is not happening.

**Organizations.** [Labor-for-housing pipeline](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#labor-for-housing-pipeline), [Tenant purchase and anti-displacement pipeline](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#tenant-purchase-and-anti-displacement-pipeline).

**Still needed.** The framework's fourth principle still says land "cannot be privately hoarded". Nothing here shows a route to that.

## Economics

*Ground every proposal in real numbers. (Issue #34)*

**Working position.** State costs and who pays, and stop citing small pilots as evidence for national schemes. The numbers below replace several in the existing drafts.

**Evidence.**
- Basic income cost: $12,000 a year to every US adult would cost roughly $3 trillion, about 75% of federal spending ([Hoynes and Rothstein](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25538), figure from the published article). A net-cost estimate for a scheme with a 50% clawback is $539 billion ([Widerquist, 2017](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bpj/bistud/v12y2017i2p13n4.html)); the two are not like for like.
- Work effects: $1,000 a month for three years reduced participants' other income by about $1,900 a year and labor market participation by 4.2 percentage points ([Vivalt et al.](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32719), current abstract). Alaska's dividend had no effect on employment ([Jones and Marinescu](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/pol.20190299)), but it is small.
- Wealth taxes: 12 OECD countries had one in 1990 and four in 2017 ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/ctp/tax-policy/role-and-design-of-net-wealth-taxes-in-the-OECD-summary.pdf)). After Norway raised its rate, some 300 very wealthy residents moved to Switzerland by 2024, while revenue still rose from 27 to an estimated 34 billion kroner ([AFP via The Local](https://www.thelocal.no/20251023/how-norways-wealth-tax-drove-out-the-rich-without-breaking-the-bank)). A proposed 2% global minimum on billionaires would raise $200-250 billion a year ([Zucman, 2024](https://taxobservatory.world//www-site/uploads/2024/06/Zucman-Report-Executive-Summary-EN.pdf)); it is a proposal.
- Debt relief: the 1953 London Agreement eliminated about half of West Germany's external debt ([Galofré-Vilà et al.](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22557)); later initiatives relieved 37 countries of more than $100 billion ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/topic/debt/brief/hipc)), with growth effects hard to isolate ([IMF](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2016/12/31/does-lower-debt-buy-higher-growth-the-impact-of-debt-relief-initiatives-on-growth-42543)).
- Citizen dividends: Mongolia's payments were based on election promises instead of actual resource earnings and were covered by borrowing ([Devpolicy](https://devpolicy.org/resources-to-cash-a-cautionary-tale-from-mongolia-20151022/)). The Marshall Islands began paying about $200 a quarter in November 2025, projected at around 8.1% of GDP ([Lowy Institute](https://www.lowyinstitute.org/the-interpreter/marshall-islands-experiment-universal-basic-income)).

**Against.** None of this amounts to a funding model. Land value capture, often proposed, has a large theoretical base but a weak evidence base: the one estimate opened is a 2007 conference paper putting US land rents at 13.6% of income ([Barker](https://www.lincolninst.edu/publications/conference-papers/urban-land-rents-united-states/)), and it measures gross rent, not collectible revenue.

**Test.** An ECONOMICS.md that cannot name, for each proposal, an order-of-magnitude cost and a payer should not be merged.

**Organizations.** [Cooperative development pipeline](SOLUTION-CIRCUITS.md#cooperative-development-pipeline).

**Still needed.** An economist. Issue #34 stays open.
